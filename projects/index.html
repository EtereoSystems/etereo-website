A 22-year-old core banking stack, migrated without a single planned outage

Banking · Core replatform · 18 months · Go, PostgreSQL, Kafka, Kubernetes, AWS

The problem — the core had accreted 22 years of undocumented behaviour, and the nightly batch no longer fit in a night. Every previous attempt had been planned as one cutover weekend, and every one had been called off.

What we did — six weeks of audit produced a dependency map and a risk register; the monolith was sliced along domain boundaries, old and new ran in parallel for eleven weeks with nightly ledger reconciliation, and 4.1M customer records moved in reversible nightly batches.

Where it landed — zero planned downtime hours, releases up from twelve a week to thirty-one, and infrastructure cost down 71%.

A dispatch product built from zero to 40 enterprise tenants

Logistics · SaaS product · 9 months · TypeScript, Next.js, ClickHouse, Terraform

The problem — the client sold dispatch software to single-depot operators and could not move upmarket. Every enterprise deal stalled on tenant isolation, per-seat billing and a missing audit trail.

What we did — multi-tenancy went into the data layer before any feature work, billing and entitlements were built as a product surface, three generations of in-cab hardware were integrated across 6,000 vehicles, and route telemetry landed in ClickHouse.

Where it landed — 40 enterprise tenants in the first year, 18% lower fuel spend, and 99.98% uptime through two peak seasons.

Clinician mobile app on a compliance-first data platform

Healthcare · Mobile + platform · 12 months · Swift, Kotlin, Go, PostgreSQL

The problem — field clinicians worked where connectivity dropped for hours and the existing app lost data when it did, while the platform could not evidence where patient records physically lived.

What we did — the app became offline-first by default with conflicts surfaced to the clinician, the audit trail moved into the write path, data residency was enforced at the storage layer with per-region keys, and iOS and Android shipped from a shared domain core.

Where it landed — 34 minutes back per clinician per day, complete audit coverage across five live regions, and zero data-residency findings in two external reviews.
